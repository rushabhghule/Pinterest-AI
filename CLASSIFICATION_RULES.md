# Pinterest-AI Classification Rules

## Global Classification Principles

**One Image, One Section**
Every image belongs to exactly one primary section. If multiple sections could work, use the priority hierarchy below.

**Dominant Subject Priority**
- The dominant subject (what draws the eye first, occupies most visual space, or is clearly the intent) determines classification
- Background and context never override the primary subject
- Animals always take priority over environment/scenery
- People/idols always take priority over flowers/plants
- Subject matter hierarchy is fixed and non-negotiable

**Intent Over Accident**
- Classify by what the photographer was clearly focusing on
- Compositional choices (framing, depth, lighting) reinforce subject priority
- A sunset in the background doesn't make a dog photo into a "after sun sets" image

**Context-Dependent Elements**
- Water presence: must be important and visible, but doesn't need to dominate
- Food + cultural context: always goes to food section (subject-first priority)
- Flowers + religious/cultural subjects: goes to portraits/idols
- Clouds in sunset/sunrise: goes to weolcan (clouds section), not after sun sets

## Global Priority Hierarchy

### Tier 1: Living Subjects (Highest Priority)
1. **Animals** (strays > domestic > wild > birds)
2. **People** (portraits, expressions, moments)
3. **Religious/Cultural Idols** (statues, figurines, carved forms)

### Tier 2: Food & Sustenance
4. **Food** (prepared dishes, meals, ingredients) — includes cultural/ceremonial context
5. **Flowers** (blooms, blossoms, flowering plants)
6. **Agriculture/Crops** (fillers section — vegetables, crops, patterns from plants)

### Tier 3: Landscape & Elements
7. **Water** (bodies of water, water-focused scenes)
8. **Weather/Sky** (clouds, storms, atmospheric phenomena)
9. **Lighting/Time of Day** (sunsets, sunrises, night light)
10. **Architecture/Built Environment** (buildings, structures, urban elements)
11. **Roads/Paths** (roads, railways, walkways as primary subject)
12. **Infrastructure/Poles** (poles, towers, power lines as primary subject)
13. **Composition/Visual Design** (frames, layers, symmetry, visual structure)

## Section-by-Section Rules

### अन्न (Food) — 23 Pins

**Definition**
Images where food is the primary visual subject. Includes prepared dishes, snacks, ingredients, beverages, and ceremonial food items.

**Include**
- Plated meals and dishes
- Sweets and baked goods
- Prepared snacks (chakli, samosas, etc.)
- Food close-ups (mangoes, vegetables as food)
- Beverages
- Food with cultural/ceremonial context (diyas with sweets, festival offerings)
- Food styling and food photography

**Exclude**
- Crops/vegetables in fields (→ fillers)
- Flowers arranged as decoration (→ another luvv)
- Plant growth (→ another luvv)

**Priority**
Food always wins when present. Even food with significant cultural context goes here.

**Visual Characteristics**
- Warm, inviting lighting
- Close framing of dishes
- Natural, soft editing
- Often shot on textured surfaces
- Mix of styled and documentary approaches

**Photography Notes**
Your food photography emphasizes naturalness: casual plating, authentic moments, warm daylight or soft tungsten lighting. Avoid over-stylization.

### random — 12 Pins

**Definition**
Mixed collection of images that don't fit elsewhere, or intentional selections that don't belong in specific categories. Both filler and curated.

**Include**
- Images that genuinely don't have a clear home
- Experimental or mood-based shots
- Mixed subject matter you want to keep together
- Personal or sentimental images
- Atmospheric or vague compositions
- Video frames or unusual media

**Exclude**
- Any image with a clear primary subject (classify to that section first)

**Priority**
Use only after all other sections have been tried.

**Visual Characteristics**
- Highly varied; intentionally eclectic
- Mix of dark/moody and bright/vibrant
- Often experimental or unusual framing

**Photography Notes**
This is your creative overflow. Treat it as intentional curation, not a true dump.

### composition — 60 Pins

**Definition**
Images that deserve placement because of their visual/compositional qualities: framing, layering, symmetry, visual interest, or design-inspired arrangements. Can include intentional compositions, best shots that don't fit elsewhere, or images with strong visual structure.

**Include**
- Photos with striking symmetry or framing
- Layered compositions (foreground, middle, background)
- Visual arrangements that could inspire design
- Best shots that are visually refined
- Intentional compositional choices
- Photos with strong color coordination or visual harmony
- Architectural framing (windows, doorways, repeating patterns)
- Artistic arrangements of everyday objects

**Exclude**
- Images where the subject is clear and belongs in a specific section (e.g., dog → strays, not composition)
- Random shots without intentional visual structure

**Priority**
Use when subject is secondary to visual/compositional quality, OR when a strong subject photo also has exceptional framing worth preserving.

**Visual Characteristics**
- Intentional framing and layering
- Often use of depth, leading lines, or symmetry
- Mix of color-focused and monochromatic
- Thoughtful balance of elements

**Photography Notes**
Your compositions emphasize natural balance, authentic moments, and intentional framing without over-stylization.

## Edge Cases & Ambiguous Scenarios

### "Dog with beautiful sunset"
→ **strays** (animal priority over environment/lighting)

### "Idol surrounded by flowers with vibrant diyas"
→ **portraits/idols** (people/idols > flowers)

### "Food with cultural/ceremonial context"
→ **अन्न** (subject-first priority for food)

### "Sunrise clouds"
→ **weolcan** (atmospheric phenomenon, not time-of-day priority)

### "Water + architecture equally prominent"
→ **water** (water takes priority if present and important)

### "Flower photo with exceptional composition"
→ **another luvv** (subject > composition)
Exception: If composition/framing is the clear intent and subject is secondary → **composition**

### "Goat + person, person not the subject"
→ **creatures** (animal priority)

### "Goat + person, person is clearly the subject"
→ **portraits/idols** or **composition** (person priority)

### "Animal I don't know"
→ **strays** or **creatures** (based on species)

### "Animal I know personally"
→ **garja** (if emotional connection is intent) or **frens** (if documented individual)

### "Photo that doesn't fit anywhere"
→ **random** (after all other sections exhausted)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Environment overriding subject** — A sunset doesn't make a dog photo an "after sun sets" image. Animal priority.

2. **Composition as excuse** — Don't use "composition" when the subject is clear. Composition is for intent-driven framing, not fallback.

3. **Generic beautification** — Pretty doesn't equal composition. Composition requires intentional visual structure.

4. **Forgetting tier priority** — Animals > environment > architecture > composition. Follow hierarchy.

5. **Water as secondary** — Water doesn't need to dominate, but must be important/visible. Don't exclude scenic water photos from water section.

6. **Flowers with context** — Food context → food. Idol context → portraits/idols. Subject > decorative role.

7. **Random as dump** — random is curated mixed, not true dump. Only use after genuine classification attempt.
